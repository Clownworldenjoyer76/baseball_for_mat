# Overall assessment

The **algebra from model probability → EV → Kelly → final selected row is internally consistent**. The primary weakness is earlier: the pipeline converts two point estimates of expected runs into full betting probabilities by assuming **independent Poisson scoring**. That distributional assumption is not learned or calibrated by the run model itself. The current production run models also underperform the DRatings run baseline on their recorded holdout tests.

Both `docs/win/baseball/mlb/04_select` and `docs/win/baseball/mlb/04_select/morning` ultimately consume the same canonical model probabilities, EVs, and Kelly values. The morning selector repeats the same probability-basis and candidate-ranking logic.

---

## 1. Home/Away Run-Mean Model

**Calculation Name/Description:** Home and Away Expected Run Prediction

**Purpose:**
Produce `model_home_runs` and `model_away_runs`, which become the fundamental parameters for every moneyline, run-line, and total probability.

**Methodology:**
Two separate `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` models use Poisson loss: one predicts home runs and one predicts away runs. Training is chronological: approximately 70% of unique dates for training, 15% validation, 15% untouched testing. Eighty-one hyperparameter combinations are evaluated using validation mean Poisson deviance; the winning parameters are refit on train+validation.

The current production feature set contains DRatings probabilities/projected runs plus starting-pitcher and bullpen features, including xERA, xwOBA, pitch-quality metrics, velocity, workload, and 7/14-day bullpen statistics. The committed production metadata does **not** list lineup, park, or weather variables among the actual model features.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The chronological split and validation design are mathematically appropriate and materially better than random splitting for time-dependent sports data.

The major issue is empirical: the committed production models were worse than their DRatings baselines on the recorded untouched test set:

* Home Poisson deviance: model **2.32477**, baseline **2.22609**
* Home MAE: model **2.35717**, baseline **2.31165**
* Away Poisson deviance: model **2.45704**, baseline **2.37451**
* Away MAE: model **2.49852**, baseline **2.42937**

The away model also predicted an average **4.3688 runs** against an actual average **3.8661**, a substantial upward bias on that test period.

Therefore the downstream probability formulas can be perfectly implemented yet still generate inaccurate probabilities because their λ inputs are imperfect.

## 2. Total Expected Runs

**Calculation Name/Description:** Expected Game Total

**Purpose:**
Provide the mean parameter for the game's total-run distribution.

**Methodology:**

$$
\lambda_{total}=\lambda_{home}+\lambda_{away}
$$

where the two λ values are the predicted home and away run means. `build_run_projection.py` writes the sum as `model_total_runs`.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The expectation identity is mathematically exact:

$$
E[H+A]=E[H]+E[A]
$$

and does **not** require independence.

However, knowing the expected total does not determine the distribution of total runs. The subsequent pipeline assumes a Poisson distribution with variance equal to that mean. That additional assumption is where probability error can enter.

---

## 3. Poisson Distribution Assumption

**Calculation Name/Description:** Conversion of Run Means into Scoring Distributions

**Purpose:**
Turn point estimates of expected runs into complete probability distributions from which win, cover, over, under, and push probabilities can be calculated.

**Methodology:**
The probability code effectively assumes:

$$
H \sim Poisson(\lambda_H)
$$

$$
A \sim Poisson(\lambda_A)
$$

with home and away scoring independent. Consequently:

$$
H-A \sim Skellam(\lambda_H,\lambda_A)
$$

and:

$$
H+A \sim Poisson(\lambda_H+\lambda_A)
$$

This is exactly what `build_juice_files.py` implements through SciPy's `skellam` and `poisson` distributions.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
This is the **largest probabilistic vulnerability in the pipeline**.

The run model is trained to estimate conditional mean runs using Poisson loss. That does **not** establish that the entire conditional distribution of actual runs is Poisson.

The downstream conversion additionally assumes:

* variance equals the mean;
* no residual home/away scoring covariance;
* no excess dispersion;
* the estimated λ values have no uncertainty.

If actual conditional scoring is overdispersed, underdispersed, or correlated, the tail probabilities used for run lines and totals will be wrong even when the expected runs are correct.

A better probability architecture would estimate the predictive distribution itself—e.g. negative-binomial/bivariate count models, simulation using empirically estimated residual distributions, or directly calibrated market-specific probability models.

## 4. Moneyline Probability

**Calculation Name/Description:** Skellam Moneyline Win Probability

**Purpose:**
Convert expected home/away runs into probabilities that either team wins.

**Methodology:**
For score difference:

$$
D=H-A
$$

the code calculates:

$$
P(H>A)=1-F_{Skellam}(0)
$$

$$
P(A>H)=F_{Skellam}(-1)
$$

and separately calculates:

$$
P(H=A)
$$

Because an MLB game must ultimately have a winner, the two non-tie probabilities are normalized:

$$
P_H=\frac{P(H>A)}{P(H>A)+P(A>H)}
$$

$$
P_A=\frac{P(A>H)}{P(H>A)+P(A>H)}
$$

so they sum to exactly 1.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The Skellam mathematics is correct under independent Poisson scoring.

Conditioning away the tie is mathematically coherent for an independent-Poisson model conditioned on a non-tied final score. It is not an explicit model of extra innings, however. The amount of tie probability assigned to each team is determined by the original Skellam probabilities rather than an independently modeled extra-inning process.

The much larger concern remains whether the Poisson/Skellam distribution is calibrated.

## 5. Run-Line Probability

**Calculation Name/Description:** ±1.5 Run-Line Cover Probability

**Purpose:**
Calculate the probability that either team covers the standard MLB ±1.5 run line.

**Methodology:**
The implementation explicitly rejects anything other than complementary `-1.5/+1.5` lines. It then applies the appropriate Skellam CDF threshold to the home score differential and sets:

$$
P_{away}=1-P_{home}
$$

The code also validates that the probabilities remain between 0 and 1 and sum to 1.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The calculation is mathematically correct for ±1.5 lines under the independent-Poisson assumptions.

Restricting the implementation to half-run lines is good: it eliminates ambiguity about pushes and prevents an incorrect binary treatment of integer run lines.

Again, the primary accuracy problem is distributional rather than algebraic. Run-line probability is particularly sensitive to the variance and shape of the score-difference distribution, making an incorrect Poisson variance assumption important.

## 6. Total Over/Under/Push Probability

**Calculation Name/Description:** Poisson Total-Run Probability

**Purpose:**
Calculate over, under, and push probabilities for sportsbook totals.

**Methodology:**
The code uses:

$$
T=H+A\sim Poisson(\lambda_H+\lambda_A)
$$

For an integer total \(k\):

$$
P(Under)=P(T<k)
$$

$$
P(Push)=P(T=k)
$$

$$
P(Over)=P(T>k)
$$

For a half-run line \(k+0.5\):

$$
P(Under)=P(T\leq k)
$$

$$
P(Over)=1-P(Under)
$$

with push probability zero. It explicitly supports integer and `.5` lines and verifies all probability identities.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The implementation is **mathematically correct** given the Poisson total assumption, including integer-line pushes.

This is one of the better-designed pieces of the pipeline. The concern is the assumed distribution:

$$
Var(T)=\lambda_H+\lambda_A
$$

is imposed rather than estimated. Incorrect total-run variance directly distorts over/under tail probabilities.

## 7. American Odds → Decimal Odds

**Calculation Name/Description:** Sportsbook Odds Conversion

**Purpose:**
Convert sportsbook prices into the decimal payoff multiplier required by EV and Kelly formulas.

**Methodology:**

Positive American odds \(A\):

$$
D=1+\frac{A}{100}
$$

Negative American odds \(A\):

$$
D=1+\frac{100}{|A|}
$$

The odds parsers prefer a supplied decimal value when available and otherwise convert American odds. The fallback conversion rounds to two decimal places.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The formulas are correct.

The fallback two-decimal rounding can introduce a small EV/Kelly error compared with retaining the exact conversion. Current selected output demonstrates that higher-precision decimals can reach the selector—for example, `-156` is represented as `1.641025641025641`.

This is minor compared with model-probability error.

## 8. Sportsbook Break-Even Probability

**Calculation Name/Description:** Offered-Price Break-Even Probability

**Purpose:**
Identify the win probability required for zero expected profit at the sportsbook's quoted price.

**Methodology:**

$$
p_{BE}=\frac{1}{D}
$$

For binary markets:

$$
Edge=p_{model}-p_{BE}
$$

For totals with push probability, the code correctly first calculates:

$$
p_{conditional}
=
\frac{p_{win}}{p_{win}+p_{loss}}
$$

and compares that probability to \(1/D\).

**Accuracy Assessment:**
Correct.

Importantly, bookmaker vig does **not** need to be removed to determine whether a bet has positive EV at the actually offered price. \(1/D\) is the correct break-even probability for that price.

De-vigging would be required if the objective were to estimate the sportsbook's fair underlying probability, which is a different calculation.

The totals implementation is also correct because pushes are excluded from the resolved-outcome probability before comparison.

## 9. Binary Expected Value

**Calculation Name/Description:** Moneyline/Run-Line Expected Return

**Purpose:**
Measure expected profit per unit wagered.

**Methodology:**

$$
EV=pD-1
$$

equivalently:

$$
EV=p(D-1)-(1-p)
$$

The same canonical model probability used by the selector is passed to this calculation.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
Mathematically exact.

The formula has no meaningful flaw. Its accuracy is completely dependent on the accuracy of \(p\).

This matters because probability error is amplified into apparently attractive EV. A probability estimate of 0.60 versus a true 0.53 can transform a normal sportsbook price into a seemingly substantial edge.

---

## 10. Push-Aware Total EV

**Calculation Name/Description:** Expected Value for Integer Totals

**Purpose:**
Correctly calculate EV when a bet can push and return the original stake.

**Methodology:**

$$
EV=p_{win}(D-1)-p_{loss}
$$

The push component contributes zero profit and zero loss.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
Correct.

Using the ordinary binary expression \(pD-1\) would be wrong for integer totals; this code does **not** make that mistake.

---

## 11. Binary Kelly Fraction

**Calculation Name/Description:** Full-Kelly Optimal Bankroll Fraction

**Purpose:**
Calculate the log-growth-optimal bankroll allocation implied by the estimated probability and price.

**Methodology:**
With:

$$
b=D-1,\qquad q=1-p
$$

the code calculates:

$$
f^*=\frac{bp-q}{b}
$$

Negative Kelly values are subsequently clipped to zero.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The formula is the standard full-Kelly solution and is mathematically correct.

However, **this is full Kelly, not fractional Kelly**.

That is important because Kelly is extremely sensitive to probability-estimation error. If a 60% estimate is really 54%, bankroll sizing can be dramatically overstated. The pipeline's most questionable component is its probability calibration, so raw full Kelly should not be interpreted as a robust staking recommendation without probability uncertainty or shrinkage.

Clipping negative Kelly to zero is logically correct for a system that does not short the opposite wager automatically.

---

## 12. Push-Aware Kelly for Totals

**Calculation Name/Description:** Kelly Fraction with Push Probability

**Purpose:**
Determine Kelly sizing for integer totals where some probability mass produces a refund.

**Methodology:**
The implementation computes:

$$
f^*
=
\frac{p_{win}b-p_{loss}}
     {b(p_{win}+p_{loss})}
$$

This is equivalent to applying binary Kelly using the conditional probability of winning given that the wager resolves.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
Mathematically correct.

The push probability scales how frequently bankroll growth/loss occurs but does not change the optimum conditional fraction beyond normalization by resolved probability mass.

The output columns retain the canonical raw win probability as `prob_for_kelly`; the internal calculation nevertheless correctly incorporates loss and resolved probability mass.

## 13. Probability-Basis Integrity Check

**Calculation Name/Description:** Selection/EV/Kelly Probability Consistency

**Purpose:**
Prevent one probability from determining selection while another probability determines EV or Kelly.

**Methodology:**
Before allowing a candidate, the selector verifies:

$$
p_{selection}=p_{EV}=p_{Kelly}
$$

within a very small tolerance and verifies that the probability-source identifiers agree.

The morning selector performs the same checks.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
This is a strong integrity control.

It prevents an important class of pipeline errors: using one probability for ranking and another for profitability calculations.

It validates **consistency**, however, not **accuracy**. Three copies of the same miscalibrated probability will pass perfectly.

---

## 14. Candidate Ranking

**Calculation Name/Description:** Best-EV / Best-Probability Candidate Selection

**Purpose:**
Choose among multiple qualifying sides for a game.

**Methodology:**
After candidates pass the numerical eligibility tests, the code can select:

$$
\arg\max EV
$$

or:

$$
\arg\max p_{model}
$$

depending on the applicable selection mode. Run-line candidates are additionally hard-rejected when EV or Kelly is non-positive.

**Accuracy Assessment:**
The ranking calculation itself is correct for its stated objective.

Statistically, maximizing a noisy EV estimate introduces **selection/optimizer bias**: the largest estimated EV is disproportionately likely to contain a large positive estimation error. There is no probability uncertainty or confidence interval in the ranking calculation.

A more robust approach would rank calibrated EV after probability shrinkage or incorporate uncertainty—for example, a conservative/lower-bound EV rather than the raw point estimate.

# Probability-accuracy conclusion

The calculations divide into two very different quality levels:

**Mathematically strong:** odds conversion, Skellam CDF arithmetic, Poisson CDF arithmetic, push handling, EV, Kelly, totals Kelly, probability-source consistency, and final CSV arithmetic. The repository also contains deterministic tests for the major probability/EV/Kelly invariants.

**Statistically weak/unproven:** the probabilities entering those formulas. The current production home and away run models underperformed their DRatings run baselines on their recorded holdout. More fundamentally, point estimates from those regressors are treated as exact independent-Poisson λ parameters without separately validating the required distributional assumptions.

The repository's latest untouched-test evaluation of a **new candidate model—not the currently committed production model** makes the risk concrete: ECE was **0.1153 moneyline, 0.1282 run line, and 0.1400 totals**, all failing the report's calibration criterion. Its positive-EV candidates averaged **+21.87% predicted EV but −5.73% realized return**, with EV/return Spearman **−0.024**.

That candidate was correctly rejected, but the current promotion gate itself is based on run-level mean Poisson deviance rather than downstream betting-probability calibration.

**Primary mathematical improvement target:** the run-mean → betting-probability transformation and calibration layer, not the EV or Kelly formulas.
